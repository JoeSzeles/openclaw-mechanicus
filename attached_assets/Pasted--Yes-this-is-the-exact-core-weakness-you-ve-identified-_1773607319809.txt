This is why simple Hebbian sugar/pain often plateaus: the reward signal has **zero gradient on magnitude**.

### The biological fix (and the one that fits your fly-brain philosophy perfectly)

In real insect and mammalian brains, **dopamine (sugar) scales with reward size**.  
A fly gets a tiny sugar drop → small dopamine hit.  
A big sugar feast → massive dopamine surge that strongly reinforces the behaviour.  
Same for punishment intensity.

We just make your existing sugar/pain mechanism **proportional to actual P&L magnitude**.  
That single change turns the brain from “happy with any profit” into a profit-maximising, loss-avoiding predator.

### Three practical ways to fix it (ranked from simplest to most powerful)

1. **Magnitude Scaling (recommended starting point — do this first)**  
   Instead of fixed +1 sugar / -1 pain:

   ``` 
   reward = actual_pips_profit / normaliser
   sugar_modifier = 1 + (base_delta × |reward|)
   pain_modifier   = 1 - (base_delta × |reward|)
   ```

   - Normaliser = e.g. 20 pips (your average expected win) or use log(1 + |pips|) for diminishing returns on huge wins.
   - Result: +50 pip winner gives 2.5× stronger sugar boost than +20 pip.  
     -30 pip loser hurts 1.5× more than -20 pip.  
   → The brain now **actively hunts bigger winners** and **fears big losers**.

2. **Risk-Adjusted Scaling (Sharpe-style — next level)**  
   `reward = pips_profit / (risk_taken_in_pips + 0.01)`  
   This punishes high-risk small wins and rewards efficient big wins. The brain learns to love high R:R setups.

3. **Long-term Survival Drive (add a “greed/hunger” layer)**  
   Every N trades, compute rolling equity curve slope.  
   If equity is making new highs → global “survival sugar” boost to the whole mushroom body.  
   This creates a built-in compounding instinct (“I want to grow fat and survive”).
